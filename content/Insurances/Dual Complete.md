# Dual Eligible Special Needs Plans (D-SNPs / “Dual Complete” Plans)

Dual Eligible Special Needs Plans — commonly called **D-SNPs** or “Dual Complete” plans — are Medicare Advantage plans designed for individuals who qualify for **both Medicare and Medicaid**. These plans combine Medicare benefits with Medicaid cost-sharing protections, creating a unified structure for members who are dual eligible.

---

## Overview

D-SNPs function as:

- **Medicare Advantage (primary payer)**  
- Coordinated with **Medicaid (secondary payer)**  

All D-SNPs must follow CMS rules, which means they retain:

- Medicare coverage criteria  
- Medicare billing requirements  
- Required MA cost-share structures (deductibles, copays, OOP limits)

Even though members rarely pay these costs, the structures must still exist.

---

## Why D-SNPs Show Deductibles & Out-of-Pocket Limits

Even though Medicaid covers patient cost-sharing, D-SNPs still display deductibles, copays, and out-of-pocket maximums. This happens for three main reasons:

### 1. CMS Requires MA Plans to Include Cost-Sharing
All Medicare Advantage plans — including D-SNPs — must:

- List deductibles  
- List copay/coinsurance amounts  
- Include out-of-pocket maximums  

These benefit structures must appear, even if the member qualifies for Medicaid.

### 2. Medicaid Acts as the Payer of Last Resort
Cost-sharing still exists **in the claim**, but Medicaid covers it on behalf of the patient.

The patient owes $0, but the system still uses the cost-share amounts to determine:
- What Medicare Advantage paid  
- What Medicaid needs to cover

### 3. Claim Processing Requires Visible Cost-Sharing
During adjudication:

1. The D-SNP applies deductible/coinsurance  
2. The system generates patient responsibility  
3. Medicaid uses that amount to determine payment  

The cost-sharing numbers must exist to coordinate benefits correctly.

---

## Billing Flow for Providers

1. **Submit claim to the D-SNP first**  
2. D-SNP adjudicates using Medicare rules  
3. Deductible/copay/coinsurance is assigned (but not billed to the patient)  
4. Medicaid pays the cost-sharing as secondary  
5. Provider receives payment reflecting both portions  

### Important Note
**If the D-SNP denies the service, Medicaid does not pay for it.**  
Medicaid only covers Medicare cost-sharing — not noncovered or denied services.

---

## Key Characteristics of D-SNP Coverage

### Medicare Rules Apply
D-SNPs must follow Medicare policies, including:

- LCD/NCD criteria  
- Face-to-face documentation requirements  
- Replacement schedules  
- Prior authorization requirements (plan-dependent)  
- Capped rental and purchase/rental rules  

### Medicaid Secondary Wrap
Medicaid typically covers:

- Deductibles  
- Coinsurance  
- Copayments  

Medicaid **does NOT** cover items denied by the Medicare Advantage plan unless the item independently qualifies under that state Medicaid program (rare for most DME).

### Prior Authorizations
D-SNPs commonly require PAs for services such as:

- CPAP/BiPAP devices  
- Wheelchairs (manual, custom, power)  
- Oxygen therapy  
- Hospital beds  
- Orthotics/prosthetics  
- Most non-routine DME  

---

## Why This Structure Exists

D-SNPs were created to:

- Improve coordination between Medicare and Medicaid  
- Protect low-income members from out-of-pocket costs  
- Reduce fragmented care delivery  
- Align financial responsibility across programs  
- Support managed care oversight for dual-eligible populations  

The result is a system where deductibles and OOP limits **must exist**, but Medicaid steps in to cover the cost-sharing.

---

## Practical Summary for Providers

- **Bill D-SNP first** — it functions as Medicare Advantage.  
- **Expect cost-sharing to appear**, but Medicaid pays it.  
- **Member responsibility is usually $0**.  
- **If Medicare Advantage denies**, Medicaid will not cover the claim.  
- **Prior authorizations are common** and often required for DME.
